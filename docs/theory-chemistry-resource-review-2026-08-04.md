# Theory chemistry resource review — Batch D

Review date: 2026-08-04

Website baseline: `7b026e14be3de4ee78b89fde8a1d65bb9f03b6dd`

## Scope and review method

This review covers General Chemistry, Physical Chemistry, Quantum Chemistry,
Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure, Inorganic Chemistry, Solid-State
Chemistry, and Surface and Interface Chemistry. Each accepted source was opened
at its official course platform or original open-publishing destination. The
review checked named instructor, author, or teaching team; visible contents;
intended level; access and licence statements where available; the missing route
it supplies; and a boundary on claims it cannot support. Provider prestige,
enrolment, and search rank were not acceptance criteria. The requested scope
excludes exercise and solution routes.

## Accepted routes

| Page | Resource and source | Learning role and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General Chemistry | Xi’an Jiaotong University, *Principles of General Chemistry*, National Smart Education Platform | Chinese first-course sequence through atomic structure, bonding, condensed states, chemical thermodynamics, equilibrium, and rates. It repairs vocabulary and macroscopic–microscopic connections, not the status of formal models as quantum observables. |
| Physical Chemistry | Roberto Peverati, *Live Textbook of Physical Chemistry*, Florida Tech / LibreTexts | CC BY-SA, author-maintained course text spanning thermodynamics, equilibrium, phase diagrams, kinetics, and quantum/molecular topics. It does not choose a calculation model or establish observable convergence. |
| Quantum Chemistry | Nanjing University, *Quantum Chemistry*, National Smart Education Platform | Chinese upper-undergraduate/graduate route from postulates and solvable systems to approximations, many-electron atoms, bonding, and computational applications. It is not a reproducible protocol for basis, correlation, or numerical convergence. |
| Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure | Blackstock, Brewer, and Cinel, *Chemical Bonding and Organic Chemistry*, Open Textbook Library | Open introductory bridge through electronic structure, periodic trends, Lewis and molecular-orbital models, geometry, and intermolecular forces. It cannot adjudicate a real-system bonding claim. |
| Inorganic Chemistry | Central South University, *Inorganic Chemistry*, National Smart Education Platform | Chinese systematic route through structure, solids, coordination and crystal-field theory, thermodynamics, and element chemistry. Formal d counts and oxidation states remain models, not unique calculated populations. |
| Solid-State Chemistry | *Fundamentals of Materials Science*, National Smart Education Platform | Chinese materials route linking crystal chemistry and defects to interfaces, phase equilibria, diffusion, transformations, and solid reactions. It is nonmetallic-material/process focused and does not validate a first-principles stability result. |
| Surface and Interface Chemistry | Dalian University of Technology, *Fundamentals of Industrial Catalysis*, National Smart Education Platform | Chinese complementary route through adsorption, diffusion, catalyst structures, and metal/oxide/semiconductor catalysts. It does not replace surface-science theory or a converged slab/reaction model. |

## Deferred sources and limits

- Unattributed course reuploads, download collections, and sources with unclear
  authorship or publisher were rejected.
- Commercial books already used as bounded references were not represented as
  open alternatives; no copyrighted scans were accepted.
- Software-only surface-build instructions, laboratory operations, and solution
  collections were not treated as independent theory-learning routes.
- Several National Smart Education Platform listings show specific session dates;
  future access, login requirements, regional availability, and platform changes
  require rechecking.

## Page mapping and remaining gaps

All additions are local to their Theory pages. The Theory index remains concise,
Reference is unfilled, and Methods is unchanged. Still-missing routes include an
open, source-clear advanced inorganic course focused on modern transition-metal
and f-element electronic structure, and a fully open course that connects
surface thermodynamics, spectroscopy, and first-principles modelling without
collapsing them into one workflow. Prose handoff points are a clearer bridge from
general bonding models to density-based analysis and a more concrete example of
why a chemical potential must be specified for surface stability.
