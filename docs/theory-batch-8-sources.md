## Electromagnetism

### MIT OpenCourseWare 8.02 — Physics II: Electricity and Magnetism

Official destination: <https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02-physics-ii-electricity-and-magnetism-spring-2007/>

Role: broad undergraduate foundation in electric fields, Gauss's law, electric potential, capacitance and dielectrics, magnetic fields, induction, electromagnetic waves, and Maxwell's equations.

Use in this page: the electronic-structure route prioritizes electrostatics, potentials, dielectrics, fields, induction, and Maxwell structure. It then narrows those ideas to Poisson solvers, Coulomb kernels, multipoles, polarization, interfaces, and boundary conditions.

Boundary: 8.02 is a full introductory course containing circuit and laboratory topics that are not all prerequisites for electronic-structure theory. It does not prescribe periodic charged-cell conventions, Coulomb truncation, potential alignment, or code-specific Poisson solvers.

## Statistical Mechanics

### MIT OpenCourseWare 8.333 — Statistical Mechanics I

Official destination: <https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-333-statistical-mechanics-i-statistical-mechanics-of-particles-fall-2013/>

Role: graduate route through thermodynamics, probability, canonical and grand-canonical ensembles, interacting systems, quantum gases, phonons, and Fermi/Bose statistics.

Use in this page: ensembles, partition functions, Fermi occupations, entropy, phonon free energies, fluctuations, and the distinction between microscopic states and thermodynamic potentials.

Boundary: the course assumes substantial physics and mathematics preparation and goes beyond the minimum required for ground-state Kohn–Sham calculations. It does not establish that an arbitrary electronic smearing parameter is a physical temperature or that one harmonic free-energy model captures all thermal degrees of freedom.

### N. David Mermin — Thermal Properties of the Inhomogeneous Electron Gas

Official destination: <https://journals.aps.org/pr/abstract/10.1103/PhysRev.137.A1441>

Role: primary formal anchor for equilibrium finite-temperature density-functional theory in the grand canonical ensemble.

Use in this page: supports the distinction between a physical finite-temperature density variational problem and numerical occupation broadening used as a convergence or quadrature device.

Boundary: the existence of a finite-temperature formalism does not select an exchange–correlation approximation, ionic free-energy model, smearing implementation, or valid temperature range for a particular material.

## Solid-State Chemistry

### MIT OpenCourseWare 3.091 — Introduction to Solid-State Chemistry

Official destination: <https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-091-introduction-to-solid-state-chemistry-fall-2018/>

Role: undergraduate entry route spanning atomic structure, bonding, crystallography, semiconductors, defects, diffusion, kinetics, and electrochemistry.

Use in this page: composition and structure types, coordination, bonding models, defects, nonstoichiometry, doping, and chemical interpretation of materials.

Boundary: the course is broad and introductory. It does not provide a complete charged-defect finite-size theory, exhaustive phase-stability workflow, advanced alloy thermodynamics, or a universal mapping between formal oxidation states and computed partial charges.

## Cross-source synthesis used by the public pages

The accepted page boundaries are:

- Electromagnetism: electrostatics, Poisson equations, multipoles, polarization, dielectric response, longitudinal/transverse distinctions, surfaces, and boundary conditions; not a universal numerical correction recipe or a replacement for full optical electrodynamics.
- Statistical Mechanics: ensembles, partition functions, occupations, entropy, finite-temperature electronic and phonon free energies, and fluctuations; not an identification of all smearing with physical temperature or one converged thermal calculation with complete thermodynamics.
- Solid-State Chemistry: structure–composition–bonding–defect relations, nonstoichiometry, doping, chemical potentials, and phase competition; not a claim that formula, relaxed structure, formal charge, zero-temperature formation energy, or convex hull alone determines synthesis and stability.
