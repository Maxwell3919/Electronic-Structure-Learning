# Theory batch 11 source review

Reviewed: 2026-08-03

Scope:

- General Chemistry
- Inorganic Chemistry
- Surface and Interface Chemistry

This record identifies the role and limitations of the sources used to delimit the three public pages. It does not reproduce textbook content, figures, tables, lecture transcripts, screenshots, or exercise solutions and does not constitute an exhaustive comparison of every course or book.

## Planning source

### Electronic Structure Atlas Theory systematic-review report

Role: classification, dependency, Tier-3 priority, candidate-resource, and scope review.

The report assigns:

- General Chemistry to atoms, periodic trends, stoichiometric awareness, bonding, molecular structure, redox, equilibrium, transition-metal awareness, acid–base language, and basic kinetics;
- Inorganic Chemistry to periodic trends, oxidation states, coordination geometry, ligand/crystal fields, d/f configurations, magnetism, Jahn–Teller effects, spectroscopy, and transition-metal electronic-structure interpretation;
- Surface and Interface Chemistry to surface energies, adsorption, work functions, surface states, interfaces, reconstruction, band alignment, dipoles, charge transfer, and low-dimensional screening.

The report classifies General Chemistry as a background-repair route rather than a compulsory prerequisite for every learner. It also records that the Inorganic Chemistry and Surface and Interface Chemistry resource sets require further dedicated review because one complete modern open course was not established.

## General Chemistry

### MIT OpenCourseWare 5.111SC — Principles of Chemical Science

Official destination: <https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-111sc-principles-of-chemical-science-fall-2014/>

Role: preferred broad background-repair route through atoms and electronic structure, chemical bonding and molecular structure, thermodynamics and equilibrium, transition metals and redox, and chemical kinetics.

Use in this page: supports the minimum chemical vocabulary needed before Quantum Chemistry, Physical Chemistry, Solid-State Chemistry, and chemical interpretation of electronic-structure results.

Boundary: the course is a full introductory chemistry course and is broader than the Atlas core intersection. It does not make Lewis structures, formal charges, hybridization, or oxidation states unique quantum-mechanical observables and does not replace convergence or method validation.

### MIT OpenCourseWare 3.091SC — Introduction to Solid-State Chemistry

Official destination: <https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-091sc-introduction-to-solid-state-chemistry-fall-2010/>

Role: materials-oriented alternative connecting chemistry foundations to crystals, defects, semiconductors, diffusion, and structure–property relations.

Boundary: it overlaps with the dedicated Solid-State Chemistry page and should not be treated as a complete quantum-mechanical or computational-method course.

## Inorganic Chemistry

### MIT OpenCourseWare 5.112 — Principles of Chemical Science

Official destination: <https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-112-principles-of-chemical-science-fall-2005/>

Role: open bridge through molecular orbitals, crystal-field theory, coordination complexes, color, magnetism, and bonding in metals and semiconductors.

Use in this page: supports the coordination, ligand-field, spin-state, and spectroscopy route needed for transition-metal electronic structure.

Boundary: it is an advanced introductory chemistry course rather than a complete modern inorganic-chemistry curriculum. Crystal-field diagrams remain idealized models and do not replace correlated, relativistic, or solid-state electronic-structure treatments.

### MIT OpenCourseWare 5.05 — Principles of Inorganic Chemistry III

Official destination: <https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-05-principles-of-inorganic-chemistry-iii-spring-2005/>

Role: graduate continuation focused on main-group synthesis, structure, bonding, and mechanisms.

Boundary: its scope is narrower than the public page and it does not independently cover the full transition-metal, coordination, magnetic, and spectroscopic responsibility.

### Kazuo Nakamoto — Infrared and Raman Spectra of Inorganic and Coordination Compounds, Part A

Official destination: <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/book/10.1002/9780470405840>

Role: spectroscopy bridge through normal modes, symmetry, force constants, inorganic applications, and DFT vibrational-frequency calculations.

Boundary: it is a paid or institutionally accessed reference and is not a first course. Spectroscopic agreement does not by itself determine a unique bonding or oxidation-state interpretation.

## Surface and Interface Chemistry

### D. Phil Woodruff — Modern Techniques of Surface Science, third edition

Official destination: <https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/modern-techniques-of-surface-science/46441C87E3A42E5E03C42BCDDFD760AF>

Role: reviewed reference for surface composition, surface structure, microscopy, surface electronic structure, and adsorbed molecules.

Use in this page: supports the experimental and interpretive context for surface states, reconstruction, adsorption, and surface characterization.

Boundary: the book is paid or institutionally accessed and emphasizes characterization techniques. It does not prescribe one first-principles slab, adsorption, electrostatic, or convergence workflow.

### Atomic Simulation Environment — surface-building documentation

Official destination: <https://wiki.fysik.dtu.dk/ase/ase/build/surface.html>

Role: implementation bridge for constructing common surface cells, adding vacuum, and placing adsorbates.

Boundary: the documentation describes geometry construction. It does not validate a surface termination, slab thickness, vacuum, adsorption site, dipole correction, energy reference, or target observable.

## Cross-source synthesis used by the public pages

The accepted page boundaries are:

- General Chemistry: chemical vocabulary and background repair; not a full laboratory or solution-chemistry curriculum and not a source of unique quantum observables.
- Inorganic Chemistry: formal oxidation and d/f counting, coordination, fields, spin states, distortions, and spectroscopic interpretation; not a claim that formal counts or crystal-field diagrams are full many-electron solutions.
- Surface and Interface Chemistry: termination, slab references, surface and adsorption energies, work functions, reconstruction, alignment, dipoles, and screening; not a universal parameter recipe or an equation between adsorption energy and catalytic activity.
