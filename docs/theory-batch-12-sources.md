## Planning source

### Electronic Structure Atlas Theory systematic-review report

Role: classification, dependency, Tier-3 priority, candidate-resource, and scope review.

The report assigns Berry Phases and Electronic Topology to polarization, Berry connection, Berry phase, curvature, gauge, Chern numbers, Wannier centres, Wilson loops, two-band models, topological invariants, orbital-magnetization awareness, and symmetry indicators. It connects the page to Brillouin-zone sampling, symmetry, localized orbitals, and relativistic spin/SOC.

The report explicitly requires gauge, Bloch-state, and occupied-subspace concepts before topology claims and rejects a page organized around visual band inversion alone. It also keeps tight-binding and Wannier methods as a cross-area responsibility rather than creating another independent directory item.

## Theory references

### Richard M. Martin — Electronic Structure: Basic Theory and Practical Methods, second edition

Official destination: <https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/electronic-structure/ED0FF348536BFFE8899627C8F78FEE6A>

Role: Atlas-wide bridge through Wannier functions, polarization and Berry phases, two-band models, topological invariants, and two- and three-dimensional topological insulators.

Use in this page: aligns terminology and the progression from Bloch-state gauge freedom to polarization, topology, and boundary consequences with the wider electronic-structure framework.

Boundary: access may require purchase or institutional authentication. It is not copied into the site and is not a complete specialized reference for interacting topological order, every crystalline indicator, or every numerical workflow.

### David Vanderbilt — Berry Phases in Electronic Structure Theory

Official destination: <https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/berry-phases-in-electronic-structure-theory/DDD71CA4FE9AF5F3A2FB300E602F394A>

Role: dedicated rigorous reference for Berry phases and curvatures, electric polarization, topological insulators and semimetals, orbital magnetization, and related response theory.

Use in this page: supports the separation of gauge-dependent connections from gauge-controlled phases and gauge-invariant quantities, the occupied-subspace formulation, polarization branches, Chern invariants, and Wannier-centre interpretations.

Boundary: the book is paid or institutionally accessed and assumes quantum mechanics and solid-state background. Its existence does not validate any particular band structure, Wannier model, symmetry indicator, or material classification.

## Advanced course context

### MIT OpenCourseWare 8.513 — Modern Quantum Many-Body Physics for Condensed Matter Systems

Official destination: <https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-513-modern-quantum-many-body-physics-for-condensed-matter-systems-fall-2021/>

Role: advanced context for geometric phases, Chern numbers, quantum Hall physics, topological phases, and interacting many-body extensions.

Use in this page: demonstrates that geometric and topological concepts extend beyond independent-electron band theory and require substantial quantum, many-body, and condensed-matter prerequisites.

Boundary: this is not a first topology course for an electronic-structure beginner. Its interacting and field-theoretic scope exceeds the page, and ordinary Kohn–Sham band calculations do not establish the many-body topological order treated in advanced continuations.

## Implementation bridge

### Wannier90 support, user guide, tutorials, and solutions

Official destination: <https://wannier.org/support/>

Role: implementation bridge for Wannier functions, interpolation, overlap matrices, centres, spreads, and calculations derived from localized representations. The reviewed support page exposes version 3.1.0 documentation and tutorial materials.

Use in this page: connects Bloch-state gauge choices, composite subspaces, overlap matrices, Wannier centres, Wilson loops, and topology-ready interpolation to a maintained software implementation.

Boundary: software documentation records implementation objects and examples. It does not independently validate projections, frozen/outer windows, disentanglement, interpolation accuracy, symmetry preservation, a computed invariant, or a material-specific topology claim. Version and actual input choices must be recorded in production calculations.

## Cross-source synthesis used by the public page

The accepted page responsibilities are:

- gauge freedom of Bloch states and composite occupied subspaces;
- Berry connection, closed-loop phase, curvature, and their different transformation properties;
- modern polarization and its branch/polarization-quantum structure;
- Chern numbers under a stated gap, filling, dimensionality, and occupied subspace;
- overlap matrices, Wilson loops, Wannier centres, Wannier functions, and localization obstruction;
- two-band models as explanatory models rather than material proof;
- symmetry, SOC, magnetic state, and bulk–boundary connections;
- observable-specific convergence and evidence requirements.

The page excludes:

- classification from a band-inversion image alone;
- identification of one edge crossing or one symmetry indicator with complete proof;
- silent use of arbitrary energy-ordered band labels at degeneracies;
- treating a Wannier interpolation or software tutorial as self-validating;
- strongly interacting topological order as a conclusion from ordinary independent-particle bands.
