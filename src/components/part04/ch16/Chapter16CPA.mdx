  <div class="bilingual-section__en" lang="en">
    <p>Consider a binary alloy $A_cB_{1-c}$. Replace every random site by a coherent-medium scatterer $t_c$, then embed one real A or B atom in that medium. Its excess scattering can be written</p>

$$
T_\alpha^{\mathrm{ex}}
=\left[t_\alpha^{-1}-t_c^{-1}-\tau_c^{00}\right]^{-1},
\qquad \alpha\in\{A,B\},
$$

    <p>or, in an equivalent cavity notation, as an impurity perturbation divided by a local denominator. Single-site CPA requires the average replacement to produce no additional scattering:</p>

$$
c\,T_A^{\mathrm{ex}}+(1-c)\,T_B^{\mathrm{ex}}=0.
$$

    <p>The coherent medium is generally complex and energy dependent, so spectral peaks acquire disorder broadening. That complex width arises from configurational averaging and should not automatically be interpreted as an electron-electron lifetime.</p>
