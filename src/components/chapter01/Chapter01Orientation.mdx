This chapter addresses a specific question: why does modern electronic-structure theory rely simultaneously on many-electron quantum mechanics and independent-particle equations? The historical sequence carries physical content. The electron, the nucleus, quantization, exchange antisymmetry, bands, self-consistency, correlation, and density functional theory each resolved a different layer of the problem. By the end, the reader should be able to distinguish an exact many-body description, an effective independent-particle model, a density-functional auxiliary system, and an approximation introduced by numerical implementation.

The prerequisites are limited to elementary quantum mechanics: the stationary Schrödinger equation, the probabilistic interpretation, basic statistical mechanics, and an initial notion of crystal periodicity. Chapter 2 surveys the material properties built from these ideas. Chapter 3 then writes the full electron–nucleus Hamiltonian and treats independent-electron approximations, exchange, and correlation explicitly.

    <dd>周期体系中随晶体动量 $\mathbf{k}$ 连续变化的一族单粒子本征值 $\varepsilon_{n\mathbf{k}}$。能带不是离散原子能级的简单复制。</dd>

## 1.1 量子理论与电子结构的起源 | Quantum Theory and the Origins of Electronic Structure

<BilingualSection titleZh="从带电粒子到稳定原子" titleEn="From charged particles to stable atoms" eyebrow="Martin 1.1 · pp. 2–3">
  <div class="bilingual-section__zh">

电子结构问题在电子被确认为物质的组成粒子之后才具有现代含义。谱线在磁场中的分裂和气体放电实验给出了电子的电荷符号与很大的荷质比；Rutherford 散射又表明正电荷集中在小而重的原子核内。经典电动力学随即遇到稳定性困难：带负电粒子在核的吸引下若按经典轨道运动，会辐射并失去能量，不能解释稳定原子和离散光谱。

Bohr 的离散轨道模型保留了量子化这一正确方向，却没有给出后来的波函数动力学。1920 年代形成的量子力学把问题改写为 Hamiltonian 在 Hilbert 空间中的本征值问题。电子不再由确定轨道描述，而由概率振幅描述；离散能谱来自算符、边界条件和可归一化要求。自旋与 Pauli 不相容原理进一步决定了多电子态的允许结构。

  </div>
  <div class="bilingual-section__en" lang="en">

Electronic structure acquired its modern meaning only after the electron was established as a constituent of matter. Magnetic splitting of spectral lines and gas-discharge experiments determined the sign of its charge and its large charge-to-mass ratio; Rutherford scattering then located positive charge in a small, massive nucleus. Classical electrodynamics immediately faced a stability problem: a negative charge moving in an attractive nuclear orbit would radiate and lose energy, contradicting stable atoms and discrete spectra.

Bohr’s discrete-orbit model retained the crucial idea of quantization but not the later dynamics of wavefunctions. Quantum mechanics in the 1920s reformulated the problem as an eigenvalue problem for a Hamiltonian acting in Hilbert space. Electrons were represented by probability amplitudes rather than definite trajectories; discrete spectra followed from the operator, boundary conditions, and normalizability. Spin and the Pauli exclusion principle then constrained the allowed structure of many-electron states.

  </div>
</BilingualSection>

<BilingualSection titleZh="相同电子与交换反对称性" titleEn="Identical electrons and exchange antisymmetry">
  <div class="bilingual-section__zh">

对相同电子交换完整坐标 $\mathbf{x}_i$ 与 $\mathbf{x}_j$ 时，物理概率不能依赖人为编号，但电子的多体波函数必须变号：

  </div>
  <div class="bilingual-section__en" lang="en">

Exchanging the complete coordinates $\mathbf{x}_i$ and $\mathbf{x}_j$ of identical electrons cannot change physical probabilities, while the electronic many-body wavefunction changes sign:

  </div>
</BilingualSection>

$$
\Psi(\ldots,\mathbf{x}_i,\ldots,\mathbf{x}_j,\ldots)
=-\Psi(\ldots,\mathbf{x}_j,\ldots,\mathbf{x}_i,\ldots).
$$

<BilingualSection compact={true}>
  <div class="bilingual-section__zh">

令两套坐标相同，交换前后函数值又必须相等，因此有 $\Psi=-\Psi$，只能得到 $\Psi=0$。这就是同一自旋轨道不能被两个电子同时占据的波函数表达。它是费米子交换对称性的结果，不是电子间 Coulomb 排斥的近似说法。即使把电子—电子相互作用设为零，反对称性仍然存在。

  </div>
  <div class="bilingual-section__en" lang="en">

If the two complete coordinates are identical, the exchanged value must also equal the original value. Antisymmetry therefore gives $\Psi=-\Psi$, so $\Psi=0$. This is the wavefunction statement that two electrons cannot occupy the same spin orbital. It follows from fermionic exchange symmetry, not from an approximate picture of Coulomb repulsion. Antisymmetry remains even when the electron–electron interaction is set to zero.

  </div>
</BilingualSection>
