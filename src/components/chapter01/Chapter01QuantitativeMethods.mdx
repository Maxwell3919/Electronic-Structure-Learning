## 1.3 定量计算的出现 | Emergence of Quantitative Calculations

<BilingualSection titleZh="自洽场把物理假设变成可迭代问题" titleEn="Self-consistency turns a physical assumption into an iterative problem" eyebrow="Martin 1.3 · pp. 7–10">
  <div class="bilingual-section__zh">

早期多电子原子计算暴露了一个结构性困难：每个电子感受到其他电子形成的平均场，而该平均场又由所有电子轨道决定。Hartree 方法将这个循环写成自洽场问题。给定初始密度，构造有效势，解单粒子方程，更新密度，再重复直到输入与输出在规定指标下相符。Fock 随后用反对称 Slater determinant 纳入交换，使单粒子方程之间出现非局域交换耦合。

自洽是方程结构，不是可靠性的同义词。一次迭代收敛只表明离散算法在给定基组、网格、边界条件、占据和泛函下找到固定点。它不证明固定点唯一，也不证明基组、$k$ 点、赝势、有限尺寸或目标 observable 已经收敛。

  </div>
  <div class="bilingual-section__en" lang="en">

Early multielectron atomic calculations exposed a structural difficulty: each electron feels an average field generated by the other electrons, while that field is itself determined by all orbitals. Hartree’s method expressed this loop as a self-consistent-field problem. One chooses an initial density, constructs an effective potential, solves one-particle equations, updates the density, and repeats until input and output agree according to a declared metric. Fock then imposed antisymmetry through a Slater determinant, introducing nonlocal exchange coupling among the one-particle equations.

Self-consistency is a property of the equation structure, not a synonym for reliability. Iterative convergence means only that a fixed point has been found for the chosen basis, grid, boundary conditions, occupations, and functional. It does not establish uniqueness or convergence with respect to basis size, $k$ points, pseudopotential choice, finite size, or the target observable.

  </div>
</BilingualSection>

<BilingualSection titleZh="基组与表示必须同时描述核附近和成键区域" titleEn="A representation must cover both core and bonding regions">
  <div class="bilingual-section__zh">

固体计算的数值难点来自空间尺度并存。核附近波函数快速振荡并具有原子特征，原子间区域则更平滑并决定成键和能带色散。早期 cellular method、augmented plane wave、orthogonalized plane wave 和 pseudopotential 思路采用不同方式压缩这种多尺度问题。

这些方法的目标是在有限数值自由度内表示相关的 Hilbert 空间子空间。选择表示后，仍需验证总能、力、能带差或其他目标量对截断参数和采样的稳定性。

  </div>
  <div class="bilingual-section__en" lang="en">

The numerical difficulty in solids arises from coexisting spatial scales. Wavefunctions vary rapidly and look atomic near nuclei, while smoother interstitial regions control bonding and band dispersion. Cellular methods, augmented plane waves, orthogonalized plane waves, and pseudopotentials compress this multiscale problem in different ways.

Their objective is to represent the relevant Hilbert-space subspace with finite numerical degrees of freedom. Once a representation is chosen, total energies, forces, band differences, or other target quantities must still be checked against cutoffs and sampling parameters.

  </div>
</BilingualSection>
