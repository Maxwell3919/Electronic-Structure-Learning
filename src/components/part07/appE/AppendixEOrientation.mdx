Appendix E specializes the general response framework of Appendix D to electromagnetic response. Chapter 21 derives optical spectra from electronic states; Chapter 20 treats phonons, Born charges, and force constants; Chapter 24 defines polarization in extended matter through Berry phases. ## 数学对象、物理对象与统一约定 | Objects, Physical Quantities, and Conventions

<BilingualSection titleZh="Gaussian 电磁单位是原书公式的基础" titleEn="Martin's formulas use Gaussian electromagnetic units" eyebrow="【Martin 原书 | Martin source】">
  <div class="bilingual-section__zh">

Martin Eqs. E.1–E.23 使用 Gaussian 电磁单位：Gauss law 含 $4\pi$，位移场满足 $\mathbf D=\mathbf E+4\pi\mathbf P$，Coulomb kernel 为 $4\pi e^2/q^2$，plasma frequency 为 $\omega_p^2=4\pi nQ^2/m_e$。这里的 $\mathbf E$ 与 $\mathbf D$ 具有相同量纲，介电张量为无量纲。

若改用 SI，基本关系变为 $\mathbf D=\epsilon_0\mathbf E+\mathbf P$，relative dielectric tensor 通过 $\mathbf D=\epsilon_0\boldsymbol\epsilon_r\mathbf E$ 定义，Coulomb kernel 与 conductivity prefactor 均发生变化。网站正文保留 Martin 的 Gaussian 公式；出现 SI crosswalk 时会显式写出，绝不静默替换。

  </div>
  <div class="bilingual-section__en" lang="en">

Martin Eqs. E.1–E.23 use Gaussian electromagnetic units: Gauss's law contains $4\pi$, the displacement field obeys $\mathbf D=\mathbf E+4\pi\mathbf P$, the Coulomb kernel is $4\pi e^2/q^2$, and the plasma frequency is $\omega_p^2=4\pi nQ^2/m_e$. In this convention $\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf D$ have the same dimensions and the dielectric tensor is dimensionless.

In SI, the corresponding relation is $\mathbf D=\epsilon_0\mathbf E+\mathbf P$, the relative dielectric tensor is defined through $\mathbf D=\epsilon_0\boldsymbol\epsilon_r\mathbf E$, and both the Coulomb kernel and conductivity prefactors change. The website retains Martin's Gaussian formulas and labels any SI crosswalk explicitly rather than replacing conventions silently.

  </div>
</BilingualSection>

Part VII uses physical fields proportional to $e^{-i\omega t}$. From $\partial_t\mathbf P=\mathbf j$,

$$
\mathbf P(\omega)=\frac{i}{\omega}\mathbf j(\omega),
$$

so Gaussian units give

$$
\boldsymbol\epsilon(\omega)
=\mathbf 1+\frac{4\pi i}{\omega}\boldsymbol\sigma(\omega),
$$

matching Martin Eq. E.11. The prose on Martin p. 602 also states a time dependence proportional to $e^{i\omega t}$; applying that statement literally together with Eq. <BilingualSection titleZh="source、response 与 inverse response" titleEn="Source, response, and inverse response" eyebrow="【定义 | Definitions】">
  <div class="bilingual-section__zh">

物质关系以总场为 source：

$$
\mathbf j_{\mathrm{int}}=\boldsymbol\sigma\mathbf E,
\qquad
\mathbf D=\boldsymbol\epsilon\mathbf E.
$$

因此 $oldsymbol\sigma$ 与 $oldsymbol\epsilon-\mathbf1$ 是对总内部场 $\mathbf E$ 的响应。反过来

$$
\mathbf E=\boldsymbol\epsilon^{-1}\mathbf D,
$$

所以 $oldsymbol\epsilon^{-1}-\mathbf1$ 是对外部-source field $\mathbf D$ 的响应。microscopic kernels 还含空间或 reciprocal indices，乘法表示 convolution/matrix multiplication；$\epsilon^{-1}_{00}$ 是 inverse matrix 的元素，不等于 $1/\epsilon_{00}$，除非所有 local-field coupling 都消失。

  </div>
  <div class="bilingual-section__en" lang="en">

The material relations use the total field as their source:

$$
\mathbf j_{\mathrm{int}}=\boldsymbol\sigma\mathbf E,
\qquad
\mathbf D=\boldsymbol\epsilon\mathbf E.
$$

Thus $\boldsymbol\sigma$ and $\boldsymbol\epsilon-\mathbf1$ respond to the total internal field $\mathbf E$. Conversely,

$$
\mathbf E=\boldsymbol\epsilon^{-1}\mathbf D,
$$

so $\boldsymbol\epsilon^{-1}-\mathbf1$ responds to the external-source field $\mathbf D$. Microscopic kernels also carry spatial or reciprocal indices and products denote convolution or matrix multiplication. The element $\epsilon^{-1}_{00}$ belongs to the inverse matrix and is not generally $1/\epsilon_{00}$ unless every local-field coupling vanishes.

  </div>
</BilingualSection>

<BilingualSection titleZh="纵向与横向 projector" titleEn="Longitudinal and transverse projectors" eyebrow="【补充定义 | Supplemental definition】">
  <div class="bilingual-section__zh">

对非零 wave vector $\mathbf q$，定义

$$
P^{L}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q)
=\frac{q_\alpha q_\beta}{q^2},
\qquad
P^{T}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q)
=\delta_{\alpha\beta}-P^{L}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q).
$$

任意 vector field 分解为 $\mathbf E_L=P^L\mathbf E$ 与 $\mathbf E_T=P^T\mathbf E$。scalar potential 产生 $\mathbf E_L\parallel\mathbf q$；transverse electromagnetic wave 满足 $\mathbf E_T\perp\mathbf q$。在严格 $\mathbf q=0$ 时 projector 不由方向唯一确定，必须指定从哪个方向、哪个 boundary condition 和哪个 $\omega/q$ 路径取极限。

  </div>
  <div class="bilingual-section__en" lang="en">

For a nonzero wave vector $\mathbf q$, define

$$
P^{L}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q)
=\frac{q_\alpha q_\beta}{q^2},
\qquad
P^{T}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q)
=\delta_{\alpha\beta}-P^{L}_{\alpha\beta}(\mathbf q).
$$

Any vector field decomposes as $\mathbf E_L=P^L\mathbf E$ and $\mathbf E_T=P^T\mathbf E$. A scalar potential generates $\mathbf E_L\parallel\mathbf q$, while a transverse electromagnetic wave has $\mathbf E_T\perp\mathbf q$. At exactly $\mathbf q=0$ the projectors are not uniquely fixed by direction; one must state the approach direction, boundary condition, and path in the $\omega/q$ limit.

  </div>
</BilingualSection>

  </div>
  <div class="bilingual-section__en" lang="en">

Martin Eq. E.5 treats $\mathbf P$ as a polarization field satisfying $\nabla\cdot\mathbf P=-Qn_{\mathrm{int}}$, while its macroscopic average retains an additive or gauge ambiguity. Martin Eq. E.20 is printed as $Z^*|e|=\partial P/\partial R|_{E_{\mathrm{mac}}}$. If $P$ is a polarization density per volume, the common code and literature convention is $Z^*|e|=\Omega\,\partial P/\partial R$. A Born-charge comparison must therefore identify whether $P$ denotes a cell dipole, a polarization density, or a quantity with the cell volume already absorbed; comparing only the numerical value is insufficient.

  </div>
